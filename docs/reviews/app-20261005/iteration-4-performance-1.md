## Findings

### P1-R4-01 [major] Closed subagent cards retain all fetched bodies outside the active conversation budget — `ui/src/lib/stores/transcript.svelte.ts:399`

**What:** `loadSubagent` stores each child's complete fetched page in `Conversation.subagents`; subsequent child pagination prepends more turns at `transcript.svelte.ts:423`. Closing the card only toggles `open` (`ui/src/modules/agents/conversation/SubagentCard.svelte:35`). There is no release/eviction of that child's body. Parent live trimming acts on `Conversation.turns`, not `subagents`. `retain()` at `transcript.svelte.ts:186` updates a saturated accounting number but does not reject or evict active bodies; whole-conversation eviction starts only after the last view releases (`:531–548`). Therefore an operator who inspects and collapses many children keeps all their payload while remaining in the parent chat. A single child's “Load earlier” also renders every accumulated turn (`SubagentCard.svelte:33,72`), bypassing the parent's 300-turn mounted window.

**Cost:** Let S be distinct child transcripts inspected in one still-open parent, P the loaded pages per child, and B the average serialized payload/page. Retention is O(S × P × B), despite cards being collapsed. Each page is normally capped to 60 turns and approximately 2 MiB by `crates/otto-transcript/src/fold.rs:34,88` (with a newest-turn exception); those are per-response bounds, not a sum across children. At only 1 MiB/page, 20 child inspections retain about 20 MiB serialized payload and 100 retain about 100 MiB, before JS objects/string representation. With tool-heavy pages approaching 2 MiB, the latter is about 200 MiB. This is a warm, plausible deep-review journey rather than background growth without user actions. The metadata cap does not prevent it: `crates/otto-transcript/src/subagents.rs:69` charges 2,048 bytes plus eight times sidecar bytes against 8 MiB; 100 small sidecars fit comfortably. For an expanded child with T accumulated turns, grouping and DOM mount are O(T), and repeated 60-turn page prepends copy a growing prefix. Nested child DOM is not included in the parent's 300-turn bound.

**Evidence: confirmed source trace; runtime magnitude unmeasured.** Followed card expand → `loadSubagent` → `setSub`, collapse, earlier-page append, `dispose`, and inactive eviction. Backend fold/cache admission bounds individual reads and server residency; it does not evict copies already delivered to this frontend map. No claim that this caused the prior RSS trend: that synthetic run did not inspect these child bodies.

**Fix:** Give child bodies explicit view leases and an aggregate byte/count LRU for released children while the parent remains active. Collapsing/unmounting a card releases its body; preserve its pagination/selection metadata and refetch evicted content on demand. Keep current visible child pages protected and apply a mounted turn window to the expanded body, with earlier/later navigation so history remains reachable. A simpler acceptable first repair is immediate release of collapsed child bodies plus bounded paging/rendering for the visible child. Retention then scales with a configured child-body budget plus currently displayed windows, rather than every child ever inspected; collapsed bodies release memory without leaving the parent conversation. Do not delete transcript files or silently discard access to earlier pages.

**Measurement/regression plan for root:** Seed one synthetic parent with 100 children and approximately 1 MiB/60-turn child pages. Open then close children sequentially, recording body-cache bytes/count and forced-GC JS heap at 0/20/50/100; parent stays mounted. Assert the released-body budget plateaus and an evicted child's content can reopen. Separately page one 1,200-turn child through 20 pages; assert mounted turn count stays within the chosen window and older/later content remains reachable. Measure expand/page interaction p95 and renderer CPU/RSS for 1 and 3 simultaneously displayed parent chats. Run on the isolated fixture only. These checks have not been executed.
